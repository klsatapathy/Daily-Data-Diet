🥗 Day 3 of 90: I spent twenty minutes yesterday trying to figure out why Python was giving me attitude, all because I tried to add my age to a piece of text. Turns out, computers are just really stubborn toddlers who throw tantrums when you mix up your apples and your oranges. 

Welcome back to the Python Pantry! If you are learning data science right now, you quickly realize that before you can build fancy predictive models, you have to understand how Python actually stores your stuff. That brings us to variables and data types.

Think of variables like labeled Tupperware containers sitting on your kitchen shelf. You slap a sticky note on a box—say, "breakfast"—and shove a bagel inside. In Python, the sticky note is the variable name, and the bagel is the value. You can change what's inside that container whenever you want, just by tossing the old stuff out and putting something new in.

Data types are simply the labels that tell you what kind of food is actually in the container. You wouldn't want to pour milk into a box meant for dry cereal, and Python feels the exact same way about its data. 

You've got whole numbers (integers, like the number of coffees I've had today—currently 3). You've got decimals for when things get precise (floats, like my bank account balance). You've got text (strings, wrapped in quotes because they love to hold hands), and true/false switches (booleans, for when you need to answer whether you're awake yet). 

If you try to treat a text string like a number, Python panics and shuts down the kitchen. Keeping your types straight is the secret recipe to writing code that actually runs without throwing red error text at you.
