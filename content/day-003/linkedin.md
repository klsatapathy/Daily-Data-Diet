I spent twenty minutes yesterday arguing with my screen because Python refused to accept that "5" and 5 are the exact same thing to my human brain.

Welcome to Day 3 of 90 on The Data Diet, where I’m raiding the Python Pantry to finally make peace with variables and data types.

Think about your actual kitchen pantry for a second. You wouldn’t dump olive oil into a wicker basket, and you wouldn’t store loose flour in a mesh bag. You use specific containers: a glass bottle for the oil, an airtight jar for the flour, and a crisper drawer for your greens.

In Python, variables are just those labeled containers sitting on your shelf. You slap a piece of masking tape on a jar, write `favorite_snack` on the front, and drop "chips and salsa" inside.

The "data type" is just Python making sure you aren’t pouring soup into a paper envelope.

• If you’re storing plain text, that’s a **String** (your grocery list).
• If it’s a whole counting number, that’s an **Integer** (buying 4 avocados).
• If there’s a decimal involved, that’s a **Float** (paying $1.75 each).
• If it’s a pure Yes/No or True/False answer, that’s a **Boolean** (are they ripe yet? True... wait, now False, you missed the window).

My big lightbulb moment today? Python isn’t being stubborn; it’s just an aggressively neat roommate. It has to know exactly what kind of ingredient it's holding so it doesn't accidentally try to multiply your grocery list by a number.

Once the pantry is labeled and sorted, cooking up the actual data recipe doesn't feel nearly as intimidating.
