Your average salary is probably lying to you. 

When I first started digging into statistics, I assumed the "average" was just a solid, honest number everyone agreed on. Then I learned how the mean, median, and mode can completely spin a story, and honestly, it felt like discovering magic tricks used against us every day. 

Think of it like a group of friends ordering dinner at a fancy restaurant. Five people are splitting the bill. Four of them ordered a basic salad, but the fifth person decided to splurge on the rarest, most expensive wagyu steak on the menu. When the bill arrives, the "mean" (what we usually call the average) skyrockets because of that one giant steak. It makes it look like everyone dropped a fortune, even though most of the table is just trying to afford the lettuce. 

That is the sneaky thing about the mean—it gets easily bullied by extreme values, or what we call outliers. 

If you want the real pulse of the group, you need the median. The median is the person sitting right in the middle of the table when you line everyone's bills up from cheapest to most expensive. It doesn’t care about the millionaire eating wagyu; it just tells you what the middle person is actually paying. And the mode? That is just the crowd-pleaser—it’s simply whatever salad was ordered the most times. 

Data works the exact same way. Depending on which metric a company or news headline chooses to show you, they can make a situation look like a total disaster or a massive success. 

Navigating these little statistical traps is my main focus today, marking Day 4 of my 90 journey learning data science in public. 

I’m curious—have you ever spotted a headline or a job salary listing where you just *knew* they used the wrong average to make things look better? Let me know in the comments!
