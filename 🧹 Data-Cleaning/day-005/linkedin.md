Your fancy machine learning model is basically an extremely confident toddler, and it will happily ruin your life if you feed it garbage.

Yesterday, I tried training a little model to predict house prices, and it completely lost its mind. It told me a broom closet in downtown was worth ten million dollars. Cue mild panic. Did my math break? Did I anger the algorithm gods? Nope. I looked under the hood and found my dataset was an absolute crime scene: missing values disguised as zeroes, stray commas, and a typo where someone apparently typed "350" instead of "3" for the number of bedrooms. 

It hit me: data cleaning isn't just the boring chores you do before the fun stuff. It is the whole game. 

Think of a data model like a world-class chef. Even Gordon Ramsay can't make a Michelin-star meal out of rotten tomatoes and old sneakers. If your ingredients are spoiled, your final dish is going to be toxic, no matter how expensive your stove is. In the data world, "garbage in, garbage out" isn't just a catchy phrase—it's an absolute law of physics. 

All that messy, inconsistent, broken information we collect every day? That's dirty data. And it silently sabotages everything from business forecasts to AI recommendations because algorithms don't have common sense. They just take whatever numbers you give them and run with them blindly. Cleaning data just means scrubbing away the human errors, filling in the blanks, and making sure everything speaks the same language before you let the computer anywhere near it. 

Tackling this on Day 5 of 90 made me realize that being a good data scientist is 10 percent cool modeling and 90 percent being a very stubborn detective.
